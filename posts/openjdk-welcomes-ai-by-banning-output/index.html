OpenJDK Welcomes AI by Banning Its Output

The safest AI integration point is outside the repository.

OpenJDK has published an interim generative-AI policy stating that contributions must not contain content generated partly or fully by large language models or similar systems.

This is delightfully Java.

Every other software project is desperately gluing an AI chatbot onto its login screen. OpenJDK looked at the same technology, formed a committee, wrote a policy document and concluded that the safest integration point was outside the repository.

You may use AI to understand the code.

You may use AI to discuss the code.

You may possibly use AI to discover which mailing list must approve your attempt to discuss the code.

But the actual patch must be produced by a human being who has signed the appropriate agreements, read the contribution guide, studied twenty years of mailing-list archaeology and learned why changing a three-character variable name requires demonstrating compatibility with SPARC.

This is perfectly reasonable.

OpenJDK already has a system capable of generating plausible-looking code that requires extensive review.

It is called a contributor.
