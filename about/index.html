Why does Angry Bile exist?

Angry Bile began, as most Java projects do, with a small problem that accidentally became infrastructure.

The problem is simple: the Java world has become far too polite.

Every framework release is "exciting." Every migration is "seamless." Every breaking change is an "opportunity to modernize." Every new abstraction reduced complexity by introducing six additional abstractions.

Nobody appears willing to say that the emperor's new reactive, cloud-native, AI-enabled trousers required seventeen annotations and still failed because of a transitive dependency from 2018.

So this site was created.

Angry Bile is a homage to a time when opinions and humour seemed to run wild and most of the time people laughed and learned from the absurdity that endured.

It is not anti-Java nor anti-change. Quite the opposite.

You do not remain angry at something for this long unless you believe it could be better.

Why anonymous?

Because the software industry claims to value candid technical criticism right up until someone actually provides it.

Many of the people writing enterprise software work for companies that sell it, support it, sponsor it, regulate it, depend on it or have somehow licensed it per employee.

Anonymity keeps the argument separate from the employer, the conference badge, the GitHub follower count and the inevitable LinkedIn post about "disagreeing constructively."

It also prevents the creation of another technology personality whose opinions slowly become a consulting business.

Judge the posts by whether they are accurate or funny.

Is this related to BileBlog?

Spiritually, yes, technically no.

Angry Bile owes a debt to the era when technical blogs had sharp edges, authors had enemies and nobody described a two-paragraph opinion as "content".
